Overview

Fifty tracks that have to agree

A cozy life-sim needs hours of music that sounds like one world and stays out of the way. A model can make any single track. The hard part is fifty that agree — when the only quality test is one person saying “this feels wrong”.

  • Me: one-sentence brief per scene, every render heard, every verdict stored word for word
  • AI agents: batch runner, analysis and ranking scripts, loudness normaliser, listening page
  • Model: Stable Audio 3 Medium, local, in ComfyUI
Scene brief→Batch job→Auto-checks→Listen & comment→Ship

Rejected takes loop back as a rewritten description. Highlighted steps are human decisions.

Three calls that changed the product
  • No loops — full three-minute pieces; long sessions need variety, not seamless repeats
  • Three ambience beds cut — too noisy, and of little use in the game
  • Every use has a place — a bright Shibuya-kei take moved from title theme to “on the way out”

What “harmonic change” measures

The track is split into short windows; each window's spectrum is folded into the 12 musical notes; the measure is how much that note profile shifts from one window to the next. A track stuck on one chord scores low; one that moves through a progression scores high.

3 · The process changed when the evidence said so

  • AI prompt expansion kept failing — it added vinyl crackle and hip-hop drums and turned “slightly uneasy” into horror. Prompts now name concrete instruments and a tempo in one sentence
  • 30-second auditions hid problems — a quiet theme passed at 30 s; all four 3-minute versions failed when a double bass entered at 1:00. Approval moved to full length
  • Percussion wouldn't behave — generate as a drumless duo, or lower the drums afterwards with source separation
Read the detail

The built-in expander added vinyl crackle, shakers and hip-hop drums, and turned “slightly uneasy” into a horror-film atmosphere that did not fit the game. From round 7, prompts named concrete instruments and a tempo in one sentence and avoided mood words like ambient or cinematic; nearly every 30-second test in that round passed.

A quiet reading theme was rated well at 30 seconds. All four three-minute versions were then rejected because a double bass came in at the one-minute mark. Quiet scenes became solo piano, and approval moved to full length.

Clap rolls and over-dense snaps could not be controlled. Two fixes: generate as a melodic duo with no drums, or remove or lower the drums afterwards with source separation, leaving the other instruments untouched.

4 · Built to ship, not to demo

  • Every render verified before anyone listens — seed, prompt, settings, real length, format; 95 jobs logged
  • One loudness standard — −18 LUFS music, −27 ambience, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP, fixed gain only
  • Provenance recorded — each shipped file maps to its render, seed and job; Stability AI Community License
  • In the game — all {{f:music.tracks}} tracks as 192 kbps MP3 with a playlist file
Read the detail

A batch runner starts one ComfyUI job per process with a 600-second limit, then decodes each file and checks seed, prompt, settings, real length and format against the job file. All 95 jobs are logged with the prompt hash and peak GPU memory.

All {{f:music.tracks}} tracks were measured to ITU-R BS.1770-4 and brought to −18 LUFS for music and −27 LUFS for ambience, true peak at or below −1 dBTP. The fix is a fixed gain only — no compression or limiting — so the sound is unchanged; six quiet tracks were left slightly under target rather than pushed through a limiter. The meter was self-tested against the standard's reference signal first.
